1980s Black And White Headshot Prompt

Silver Gelatin Studio Headshot · the full 1980s black and white headshot prompt. Upload a photo of yourself to ChatGPT or Gemini, paste this prompt and get your 80s version.

Monochrome headshots from the decade were what actors, musicians and graduating students handed out, and they still look serious and flattering without trying. Removing colour also removes a lot of the tell-tale signs of AI editing, so this is one of the most believable ways to see your own face in a period portrait.

Best with: ChatGPT or Gemini

Prompt

Using my uploaded photo, turn me into a 1980s black-and-white studio headshot. Preserve my face, facial features, bone structure, skin texture, age and proportions so I am clearly recognisable; do not beautify or airbrush. Give me a period hairstyle that suits my hair type, such as a soft side-swept cut, a layered bob, or short tapered sides with volume on top, and dress me in a dark crewneck sweater or a shirt with a narrow tie. Pose three-quarter to camera, chin slightly lowered, eyes to the lens. Light me with one large key light from camera left and a small reflector, so one cheek falls into gentle shadow against a mottled grey canvas backdrop. Shot on medium-format black-and-white film and printed on fibre silver-gelatin paper: fine even grain, deep blacks, creamy highlights, a faint white border. No colour, no digital sharpness, no text or logos, not an illustration.

What this prompt creates

The result is a quiet three-quarter headshot on a mottled grey canvas background. A single soft light from the left models your face, leaving one cheek in gentle shadow. You wear a dark sweater or narrow tie and a period haircut suited to your hair. The tones run from deep black to creamy white with fine, even grain, framed by a thin white paper border.

How to use this 1980s black and white headshot prompt

  1. Tap Copy prompt above to copy the full text.
  2. Open ChatGPT or Gemini and start a new chat. Upload a clear, well-lit photo where your face is fully visible (no sunglasses or heavy filters); for couple or family prompts, upload a photo with everyone in it.
  3. Paste the prompt and send it. Replace the example hairstyles and the sweater or tie with the cut and clothing you would actually have chosen for a studio sitting.
  4. If the face changes, reply “keep my face exactly as in the uploaded photo” and regenerate. Try two or three versions and keep the one that looks most like a real old print.

Tips for this prompt

  • Upload a well-lit colour photo with visible skin texture; heavily filtered selfies give the model nothing to preserve and the headshot will look plastic.
  • If the face drifts, regenerate with "keep my exact eye shape, nose and jawline from the uploaded photo" added right after the identity sentence.
  • Move the key light to camera right, switch the backdrop to plain black, or ask for a slightly warmer selenium-toned print for variety.

Who this prompt suits

Anyone who wants a serious, timeless portrait rather than a costume, including performers, writers and professionals looking for a distinctive profile picture or a framed print for a portfolio page.

What makes this prompt work

"Bone structure, skin texture" is added to the usual identity lock because black and white exaggerates shape and texture, so both must stay yours. "One large key light from camera left and a small reflector" gives a clear lighting direction instead of flat grey. "Fibre silver-gelatin paper" and "creamy highlights" steer the tonal feel, while "no digital sharpness" and "not an illustration" stop crisp, over-processed results.

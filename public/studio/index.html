Featured studio portrait prompts

  • Mid-Eighties Master Portrait – This is the all-purpose version of the trend, for anyone who simply wants to know how they would have looked in the mid-1980s.
  • College Yearbook Portrait – Yearbook portraits from the 1980s are a genre of their own: the same head tilt, the same flat friendly light, and the unforgettable laser or mottled backdrop.
  • Moustache And Sweater Vest Portrait – The moustache-and-sweater-vest portrait is the picture that sat framed on a thousand living-room shelves: slightly formal, a little shy, very sincere.
  • Soft-Box Glamour Studio Portrait – The glamour portrait was the decade's special-occasion photo: big hair, silk, pearls and that dreamy soft-focus glow. It feels polished but not modern, which is exactly why it is popular.
  • Balloon Arch Prom Night – The prom portrait under a balloon arch is a rite-of-passage photo, and the mid-eighties versions are gloriously over the top. Ruffles, puffed sleeves and a pastel tux make it one of the most fun couple edits to try.
  • Old Passport and ID Picture – An old ID picture is the least glamorous photo anyone owns, and that is exactly the charm.
  • Silver Gelatin Studio Headshot – Monochrome headshots from the decade were what actors, musicians and graduating students handed out, and they still look serious and flattering without trying.
  • Mall Studio Laser Glamour Shot – The laser-beam backdrop was the most over-the-top option at the portrait counter, and people chose it on purpose.

Lighting a studio portrait prompt the 80s way

Yearbook and ID photos used flat, even light from the front. Ask for it explicitly or the model will add dramatic modern shadows.

Glamour studios used a big soft box, a hair light and heavy diffusion. Words like soft focus and airbrushed glow push the result in that direction.

Backdrops matter as much as clothes: mottled grey or blue canvas, laser beams or a pastel gradient each date the image at a glance.
